                    🔬 Genetics & Assays Science Guide
                    RESEARCH CONTEXT · PREVIEW · v0.4 SUPPORT
                

                
            

            

                

                    This guide has three parts, and they must not be mixed up.
                    Part A is what published experiments on real flies report; none of it is NeuroFly output.
                    Part B is the engineered browser preview, which is illustrative.
                    Part C is what this release actually supports. No circuit computation (learning, compass navigation,
                    escape decisions, wind tracking, circadian rhythm or courtship) has been demonstrated in the NeuroFly connectome.
                


                
                

                    
Part A Published fly research: context, not model output

                    

                        To ask whether a cell type is necessary for a behaviour, fly neuroscientists silence it genetically and compare
                        the flies with controls in a standard assay. Common tools are the GAL4/UAS expression system and effectors such as
                        tetanus toxin light chain (TNT), shibirets and Kir2.1
                        citation needed: primary sources for these tools are not recorded in this repository.
                        For example, von Reyn et al. (2014) compared giant-fibre split-GAL4 × UAS-Kir2.1 flies with genetic controls
                        (source in the table). Silencing evidence shows necessity in that assay; it does not by itself show how the circuit computes.
                    

                    

                        Numbers below are published fly results, quoted with the uncertainty the source gives. They are not targets
                        for NeuroFly and NeuroFly has not reproduced them. "Read" says how much of the source this project has checked.
                    

                    

                        
                            

                                Circuit / assay
                                Reported in flies
                                Source (how it was checked)
                                Uncertainty
                            

                        
                        
                            

                                Mushroom body, odour–shock learning (T-maze)
                                After odour–shock pairing, "typically, 95% of trained flies avoided the shock-associated odor".
                                Tully T, Quinn WG (1985) J Comp Physiol A 157:263–277. doi:10.1007/BF01350033. Read: abstract only.
                                The paper's exact performance indices were not read; a PI derived from the 95% figure is approximate. Which mutants and lesions abolish this learning: citation needed.
                            

                            

                                Dopamine-instructed KC→MBON plasticity
                                One pairing of a 1-s odour with PPL1-γ1pedc dopamine-neuron activation cut MBON-γ1pedc's response to that odour by 80 ± 5.7% (unpaired odour: 27 ± 7.1%).
                                Hige T, Aso Y, Modi MN, Rubin GM, Turner GC (2015) Neuron 88:985–998. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2015.11.003. Read: Fig. 1. Compartment differences: Aso Y, Rubin GM (2016) eLife 5:e16135, doi:10.7554/eLife.16135 (read).
                                One output-neuron type; the dispersion measure behind ± is not recorded in this project's notes. Other compartments learn at different rates (Aso & Rubin 2016).
                            

                            

                                Naive odour avoidance (T-maze, no training)
                                Wild-type w1118 (iso1CJ) avoidance index against air at training dilutions: OCT 54.92 ± 3.43, MCH 58.33 ± 6.79.
                                Li W, Cressy M, Qin H, Fulga T, Van Vactor D, Dubnau J (2013) J Neurosci 33(13):5821. doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4004-12.2013. Read: Methods, Results, Fig. 1I table.
                                Mean ± SEM, N ≈ 6–10 groups; the index rises at 10× concentration. Only the sign is used by NeuroFly's preregistered (not yet run) T-maze spec.
                            

                            

                                Giant fibre (DNp01) looming escape
                                A GF spike forces a takeoff, and every short-mode (GF-mediated) takeoff needs a GF spike. The fraction of short-mode takeoffs falls with log(r/v) (R² = 0.903). GF spikes and GF-mediated takeoffs occur on average at a fixed delay after a looming disk passes 39°.
                                von Reyn CR, et al., Card GM (2014) Nat Neurosci 17:962–970. doi:10.1038/nn.3741. Read: main text pp. 962–963, figure captions, supplement; Methods not read. von Reyn CR, et al., Card GM (2017) Neuron 94:1190–1204. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2017.05.036. Read: full text.
                                n = 102–202 flies per r/v. In controls only 95 of 638 flies made short-mode takeoffs at r/v 14–70 ms; "looming stimuli generate GF spikes in only a small subset of tethered flies" (2017).
                            

                            

                                Optomotor response
                                Flies turn with wide-field rotation. In flight, responses rise steeply to a plateau at 3–10 Hz temporal frequency. Walking flies (60° wavelength, 1 Hz) turn with high-contrast gratings for about 1 s, then reverse.
                                Duistermars BJ, Chow DM, Condro M, Frye MA (2007) J Exp Biol 210(18):3218. doi:10.1242/jeb.007807. Read: full text. Mano O, et al., Clark DA (2023) eLife 12:e86076. doi:10.7554/eLife.86076. Read: full text.
                                Depends on flight versus walking, wavelength, temporal frequency and contrast; the turning direction can reverse during long stimuli.
                            

                            

                                Motion detectors (T4/T5)
                                T4/T5 subtypes a–d respond to front-to-back, back-to-front, upward and downward motion.
                                Maisak MS, et al., Borst A (2013) Nature 500:212–216. doi:10.1038/nature12320. Read: abstract only.
                                In NeuroFly this tuning is imposed by an engineered encoder, not computed by the connectome (Part C).
                            

                            

                                Steering descending neurons (DNa02)
                                DNa02 activity predicts turning toward its own side.
                                Rayshubskiy A, et al., Wilson RI (2025) eLife 13:RP102230. doi:10.7554/eLife.102230. Statement carried from this project's studio notes; not re-read for this guide.
                                Absolute firing rates appear in figures only; reported burst rates are unverified.
                            

                            

                                Central complex heading
                                Heading representations in the central complex are reported to stay stable across diverse visual scenes.
                                Kim SS, Hermundstad AM, Romani S, Abbott LF, Jayaraman V (2019) Nature 576:126–131. doi:10.1038/s41586-019-1767-1. Retrieved 20 Sep 2026; title-level claim.
                                The E-PG "compass bump" and PFL3 steering papers are not recorded here: citation needed.
                            

                            

                                Visual place learning (heat maze)
                                Flies can learn to find a cool spot using visual landmarks.
                                Ofstad TA, Zuker CS, Reiser MB (2011) Nature 474:204–207. DOI not recorded in this repository; cited only for the existence of the behaviour.
                                No performance number is quoted. Its 5-minute trials allowed leaving the cool tile, unlike NeuroFly's heat maze.
                            

                            

                                Odour plume navigation (wind tunnel)
                                Odour onset evokes upwind movement; odour offset evokes search.
                                Álvarez-Salvado E, et al. (2018) eLife, article 37815 (elifesciences.org/articles/37815). Recorded in this project's behaviour review; not re-read for this guide.
                                Treat as a lead until re-read.
                            

                            

                                Buridan stripe fixation; Johnston's organ wind and sound sensing; s-LNv/PDF circadian clock; P1 and cVA courtship circuits; gap-crossing leg reach
                                Described in the fly literature.
                                citation needed — no verified primary source is recorded in this repository.
                                Not stated quantitatively here until sourced.
                            

                        
                    

                


                
                

                    
Part B Engineered modular preview: illustrative, not connectome results

                    

                        Without a daemon the dashboard runs a small hand-built JavaScript model: 120 Kenyon-cell units, a 16-wedge heading compass,
                        locomotion and reflex modules. The daemon's modular backend is also hand-built, with explicit assay reflexes.
                        Neither is the connectome. Their behaviour has not been compared with fly data.
                    

                    

                        The genotype buttons are named after the experiments in Part A, but each one only switches off a part of the
                        browser preview. They are not genetic interventions on the connectome, no deficit size is predicted, and they are
                        disabled whenever a daemon drives the view.
                    

                    

                        
                            

                                Button
                                What it switches off in the preview
                                Connectome lesion
                            

                        
                        
                            

                                WT Control
                                Nothing; all preview modules run.
                                The connectome runs unmodified; there is no genotype to choose.
                            

                            

                                ΔMB (Kenyon)
                                The preview's Kenyon-cell learning update stops. Kenyon-cell activity is still computed.
                                Planned — not in v0.4 (NEXT-02, NEXT-03)
                            

                            

                                ΔCX (Compass)
                                The preview compass is replaced by random jitter, so its steering term becomes random.
                                Planned — not in v0.4 (NEXT-02)
                            

                            

                                ΔGF (Escape)
                                A looming trigger no longer starts the preview's escape.
                                Planned — not in v0.4 (NEXT-02)
                            

                            

                                ΔJO (Wind)
                                In the Open Arena preview only, the wind-direction input to the preview compass is set to zero. The Wind Tunnel and Sandbox previews ignore this button.
                                Planned — not in v0.4 (NEXT-02)
                            

                        
                    

                


                
                

                    
Part C What v0.4 actually supports

                    

                        Connectome. The MaleCNS v1.0 graph runs unmodified: 166,700 neurons and 25,582,938 edges, with no connections added.
                        No paradigm has reached "Tested on v3". Optomotor is "Provisional on v3, input-imposed": the encoder imposes direction
                        selectivity, so it is not evidence that the connectome computes the behaviour. The other 13 paradigms are
                        "Mapped, untested on v3". No circuit computation has been demonstrated. The canonical capability matrix ships with
                        the package; print it with neurofly capability.
                    

                    

                        Connected controls (Assay Tools & Levers) change the stimulus or environment, and the daemon acknowledges
                        each change. They do not make the model learn and do not set a behaviour.
