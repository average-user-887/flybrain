## 10.1 Why v6a exists: the signal collapses at the first synapse, and the code says why

Under v4, the v5 primary and the v5 upper-kinetics arm alike, the per-cell F1
amplitude at the grating frequency (`v5_raw/*.analysis.json`, D2, left eye)
falls from R1–R6 ≈ 9.57 mV to L1 ≈ 0.47, L2 ≈ 0.20, L3 ≈ 0.18, to Mi1 ≈ 0.05,
Tm3 ≈ 0.08, Mi9 ≈ 0.05, Tm1 ≈ 0.07, Tm2 ≈ 0.08, Mi4 ≈ 0.015, and to T4/T5
≈ 0.02 mV. The fall is identical within a few percent under all three
kinetics tables, so it is not synaptic timing. The better-lit right eye is
about twice as large at every stage and shows the same profile.

**The mechanism, checked in the code before relying on it.** At the engine's
small-signal operating point, the DC voltage gain of one graded synapse is

    dV_post/dV_pre = (dr/dV_pre) · W · g_unit · τ_ref/1000 · |E_syn − V_post| / g_tot,post

where `dr/dV = r_max/(E_exc − E_inh) = 6.49 s⁻¹ mV⁻¹` is the slope of v4's
linear release function (§7.3), `W` is the summed weight from in-phase
presynaptic partners, `g_unit` the per-sign quantum, and `τ_ref = 5 ms` (v5's
charge-preserving quantum makes the time-averaged conductance per release rate
independent of the kinetics table, so this holds for every arm). For an L1/L2
cell that receives photoreceptor input at all, the pinned graph gives a mean
`W` of **35.76** from R1–R6 (1,623 of 3,555 L1/L2 cells; the other 1,932 receive
no R1–R6 synapse in the reconstruction). With only its leak and the tonic
photoreceptor conductance that this weight implies at the photoreceptor
operating point, the formula gives **0.147**. The measured in-network numbers
agree with it. The right-eye 90th percentile of the L1 per-cell F1 under v5
is 1.6 mV against 9.5 mV in R1–R6, i.e. 0.17 for the best-connected cells. For
L1→Mi1, the hop gain is ≈ 0.1. **The task's premise is confirmed with one
correction.** The 10 mV photoreceptor modulation changes release by about 34 %
of its tonic value, not 14 %, because the photoreceptors sit at −42 mV under
the mean drive. The deficit is still the product above. The release slope is
the only factor in it that is not a connectome weight or an already-declared
constant. Each graded hop multiplies the signal by ≈ 0.1–0.4. Three to four
graded hops separate the photoreceptors from T4/T5, which accounts for the
≈ 500× collapse.

**In the animal the first synapse amplifies.** Laughlin, Howard & Blakeslee
(1987), blowfly: the characteristic curve relating photoreceptor and LMC
response amplitudes "is sigmoidal", "the shape and slope of the characteristic
curve is unaffected by the state of light adaptation", and retinal antagonism
keeps it "centred on the mean level of receptor response generated by the
background. Thus the photoreceptor synapses operate in the mid-region of the
curve, where the slope or gain is highest and equals approximately 6." The
engine's 0.147 is **41× below** that.

v6a tests one hypothesis: **restore the measured gain of the one graded synapse
whose gain has been measured, apply it by class, and see whether the signal
then reaches T4/T5 at a biological scale and whether the connectome then
computes direction.**

v1–v5 are not deleted, superseded or reinterpreted. All stay selectable, keep
their pins (v1 `1b17bfc6…f146`, v2 `1ea33ba1…eb56`, v3 `5739c4f4…4a41`, v4
`7ae09665…ed44`, v5 `08ff5652…10f4`), stay reproducible, and every number
published under them stands. The default stays v3.

The input-timing blocker (Tm3 leads Mi1 in the animal, Behnia *et al.* 2014;
the model has it backwards, `WP5_OPTOMOTOR.md` §14.4) is **v6b's job and is not
addressed here.** v6a adds no cell-type filter. It reports the timings it
observes (D4).

**No scalar *Drosophila* photoreceptor → LMC voltage gain is published.**
Nikolaev *et al.* 2009 state explicitly that the gain "cannot be estimated
simply by comparing their maximum amplitudes". Their *Drosophila* gain is
frequency-dependent and light-level-dependent, and is a ratio of response
spectra that contains LMC and network dynamics, not only the synapse. The
declared value is therefore **a cross-species transfer of the blowfly
characteristic-curve slope, 6**. It is chosen over Weckström & Laughlin 2010's
×10–12 because that figure includes the extracellular field-potential
mechanism, which this engine does not have and which would be counted twice.
It is chosen over the *Drosophila* spectral ratios because those are not a
property of the release function alone. They include the LMC's own band-pass
dynamics, which is v6b's territory.

**Lamina → medulla transfer: none exists.** No measured voltage gain or
input–output curve for L1→Mi1, L2→Tm1/Tm2, L3→Mi9 or any other
lamina → medulla synapse was found in any fly. Other insect graded synapses,
recorded for completeness:

* Locust ocellar L1–3 → L4/5, a tonic graded synapse: "for presynaptic
  potentials between −4 and +4 mV, there was a linear change in postsynaptic
  potential of 0.7 mV for each 1 mV change in presynaptic potential"; gain

  PMC6725398, FULL).
* Lobster LP → PD: a sigmoid with slope factor k = 5.2–9.3 mV (Manor *et al.*
  1997, *J Neurosci* 17:5610, PMC6793824, FULL).
* Locust non-spiking interneurones: release with ≈ 2 mV of depolarisation in
  some cells and tonic release at rest in others (Burrows & Siegler 1978,
  ABSTRACT).

## 10.3 The transfer model

**Form: a linearised slope about a declared anchor, with v4's bounds.** For
presynaptic release class `c`:

    r_c(V) = clip( r_v4(V_A,c) + G_c · r_max/(E_exc − E_inh) · (V − V_A,c),  0,  r_max )

* `r_v4(V) = r_max (V − E_inh)/(E_exc − E_inh)` is v4's release (§7.3).
  **v6a keeps v4's release value at the anchor**, i.e. v4's maintained
  baseline (§7.4), and changes only the slope there.
* `G_c = 1` gives v4's line exactly, for any anchor. **v6a with every `G = 1`
  is v5.**
* The clip bounds are v4's own: release is never negative and never exceeds
  the refractory ceiling `r_max = 454.5 s⁻¹`.
* This is the piecewise-linear stand-in for Laughlin's sigmoid. It has the
  measured mid-region slope and the measured saturation at both ends, but kinks
  where a sigmoid is smooth. It is an engineering simplification, declared. Its
  merit is that it adds no shape parameter and nests v5 exactly.

**Classes, by the presynaptic neuron's `cell_type`, applied brain-wide.** The
release function belongs to the release site. A class's gain applies to every
out-edge of every neuron of the class, onto any target. The graded set is v4's
declared policy `v4-optic-lobe-graded` (95,545 neurons), unchanged. No cell
list is introduced beyond it.

| class | members | G | V_A | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `photoreceptor_R1-R6` | `cell_type == 'R1-R6'` (3,377) | **40.691** (derived, below) | **−42 mV** | calibrated to a measured synaptic voltage gain (blowfly; cross-species) |
| `default` | every other declared graded neuron (92,168) | **1** | (−52 mV; irrelevant at G = 1) | **DECLARED DEFAULT: no measured transfer, v4/v5's line kept** |

R7/R8 are in `default`. Their synapses' gain is unmeasured, and the
photoreceptor encoder does not drive them, so they would not sit at the R1–R6
anchor.

**The photoreceptor anchor `V_A = −42 mV` is `V_rest` plus the encoder's mean
drive `i_max/2 = 10` mV-equivalent.** It is the potential of an isolated
driven R1–R6 under the declared adapting background, computed from declared
constants and not measured on the network. Laughlin 1987 is the reason to
anchor at the background-adapted level rather than at rest. The synapse is
re-centred "on the mean level of receptor response generated by the
background". v6a has no adaptation, so the anchor is fixed at the declared
background. A different stimulus background would need a different anchor.
That is a stated limitation, and nothing here is re-anchored per run.

**Why the default is 1 and not a cross-system number.** Three reasons, fixed
before measuring:

1. No lamina → medulla, or any other optic-lobe graded synapse past the
   photoreceptor, has a measured voltage transfer (§10.2).
2. A gain above 1 needs an anchor. A non-photoreceptor graded cell's operating
   point is not a declared constant: under v5 the medulla columnar cells sit at
   −54 to −63 mV in gray, 2–11 mV below `V_rest`. Any fixed anchor would
   silence some cells (release clipped to zero) and saturate others. That is a
   new free choice per cell type, and making it per cell type would be the
   hand-built adapter the owner's constraint excludes. v4's line through the
   membrane bounds is the only release function in this family that needs no
   anchor.
3. The locust ocellar measurement (0.7 mV/mV) is a different system, a
   different synapse and an excitatory one. Transferring it would add a number
   whose only role would be to raise downstream amplitudes, the very outcome
   under test.

The consequence is stated as a prediction (§10.6). If v6a fails because the
signal still dies downstream of the lamina, the deliverable names the missing
measurement.

## 10.4 Derivation of the photoreceptor gain

Reference cell: an LMC with leak, the tonic photoreceptor conductance, and
nothing else. It receives the mean photoreceptor weight of a photoreceptor-
connected L1/L2 cell, `W_ref = 35.7603` (1,623 cells under the v3 transmitter
policy; median 34.38), from photoreceptors that all carry the same signal (in
neural superposition, one cartridge's R1–R6 share an optical axis). Histamine is
inhibitory: `g_unit_inh = 1/18`, `E_inh = −70 mV`. At the anchor:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| `r(V_A) = r_max (−42 + 70)/70` | 181.82 s⁻¹ |
| tonic conductance `g0 = W_ref · g_unit · τ_ref/1000 · r(V_A)` | 1.8061 leak units |
| reference LMC potential `(V_rest + g0 E_inh)/(1 + g0)` | −63.59 mV |
| `dV_post/dr = (V_post − E_inh)/(1 + g0) · W_ref g_unit τ_ref/1000` | 0.022708 mV per s⁻¹ |
| v4 small-signal gain `= dV_post/dr · 6.4935` | **0.14745** |
| **`G = 6 / 0.14745`** | **40.691** |

The resulting release reaches zero at −42.69 mV and `r_max` at −40.97 mV. The
calibrated photoreceptor synapse is linear over a **1.7 mV** presynaptic window.
Weckström & Laughlin 2010 put ≈ 1.5 mV "well within" the operating range, so
the window is the right order but narrow. **Stated tension:** at the anchor,
the engine's relative slope corresponds to an e-fold per 0.69 mV, against
Laughlin 1987's 1.50–1.86 mV per e-fold in postsynaptic conductance. Matching
the measured voltage gain on this graph's weights needs release ≈ 2.4× steeper
than the animal's, because the engine's tonic conductance per mV is smaller
than the animal's. This is reported as a property of the weight scale (0.275 mV
per synapse-event, Shiu *et al.* 2024), not hidden in the gain.

## 10.5 Identity, pins, state, arms

* `dynamics_version = 'v6a'`, controller `brainlab-lif-v6a`. The declared dict
  is `graph_identity.LIF_DYNAMICS_V6A`, with pin `dynamics_pin('v6a')`. v1–v5
  dicts and pins are byte-unchanged (tested). The default stays v3.
* `Brain(..., dynamics='v6a', kinetics=<v5 table>, release=<table>)`. Tables
  live in `brainlab.graded_release.RELEASE`: `v6a-calibrated` (primary) and
  `v5-linear` (every G = 1). The per-neuron class assignment and the table are
  hashed (`release_sha256`) into every snapshot. A snapshot is refused under
  another version, graded set, kinetics hash or release hash.
* CPU reference kernel `engine.advance_v6a`; GPU `cupy_v6a.CupyV6aState`. It
  subclasses v5's GPU state, so `cupy_v5.py` is not edited. The kernel
  evaluates `x = clip((V − rel_v0)·rel_k, 0, r_max·dt/1000)` per neuron, with
  `rel_v0 = V_A − (V_A − E_inh)/G` and `rel_k = G · r_max dt/1000/(E_exc − E_inh)`.
  With `G = 1`, `rel_v0 = E_inh` and `rel_k` are exactly v5's scalars, so the
  arithmetic is v5's.

**Declared arms. These two only.**

* **Primary:** v5 primary kinetics (`v5-receptor-class`) + `v6a-calibrated`.
* **Arm K0:** v4-equivalent kinetics (`v4-equivalent`, every τ = 5 ms) +
  `v6a-calibrated`. It separates the effect of gain from the effect of kinetics.
  K0 against v4, and the primary against v5 primary, are the two like-for-like
  gain comparisons.

No other table, gain, anchor or default is run. If both arms fail, the answer
is that they failed. No third table is tried and no slope is adjusted.

## 10.6 Predictions (made before any v6a measurement)

* **P1 (certain by construction, checked):** the two-cell transfer probe gives
  a small-signal gain of −6.0 (±2 %) under `v6a-calibrated` and −0.147 under
  `v5-linear`, in both kinetics arms. `v5-linear` reproduces v5 bit for bit on
  synthetic graphs (CPU and GPU). On the real graph it reproduces v5 within
  v5's own run-to-run float tolerance (§14.6 of WP5 found that v4 and v5 are not
  bit-reproducible run to run on this GPU).
* **P2 (high): the lamina reaches a biological scale.** Right-eye p90 of the
  per-cell F1 rises from 1.6 (L1) / 0.72 (L2) mV under v5 to **3–15 mV**. The
  reference two-cell probe gives ≈ 9 mV for the 9.57 mV photoreceptor
  modulation, saturation-limited. In-network the LMC has other conductances
  (C2, C3, L5, Mi1, Tm3, Dm cells), which lower it.
* **P3 (moderate-high): the signal still collapses after the lamina.** The
  default class keeps v5's per-hop gains of ≈ 0.1–0.4, so medulla and T4/T5
  scale up by roughly the lamina's factor and no more. **Predicted right-eye
  T4 and T5 p90: 0.2–0.8 mV**, below the T4/T5 band, so the **stage gate is
  predicted to FAIL on its T4/T5 clause.**
* **P4 (moderate): network health holds.** The primary's gray brain mean rate
  stays inside 0.5–25 Hz, near v5's 10.4 Hz (K0 near v4's 1.2 Hz). Under 5 % of
  graded cells sit at a membrane bound, and DNa02 fires in gray. The tonic
  release at the anchor is v4's. R1–R6 in gray spread over about ±1 mV around
  −41.7 mV (v5), against a 1.7 mV linear window, so **a substantial fraction of
  photoreceptor release will sit clipped at 0 or r_max in gray**. That is
  reported, not gated.
* **P5 (high): no direction selectivity, if the motion gate is reached.** The
  gain adds no space–time inseparability. The input timings are v5's, and
  §14.4 showed they are wrong. The clip adds a nonlinearity, and a D1 response
  could arise from it, but it is not predicted to be subtype-specific.
* **P6 (moderate): K0 and the primary agree on every gate verdict.**
* **P7 (timing, reported for v6b):** flicker delays stay within 10 ms of v5's.
  The Mi1/Tm3 order stays reversed relative to Behnia 2014. Gain does not move
  phase in a linear regime, and saturation may advance it.

## 10.7 Falsifiers

Each is reported whichever way it comes out. None licenses changing G, an
anchor, the default, a band, the class map or the protocol.

* **F1:** the unit probe does not give −6 ± 2 %, or `v5-linear` does not
  reproduce v5 (synthetic: bit-identical; real graph: identical spike counts
  and max |ΔV| ≤ max(v5's own run-to-run difference, 1e-4 mV)). This means an
  implementation error.
* **F2:** the lamina misses its band in-network although the unit probe
  passes. The calibrated synapse is then shunted or saturated by the
  connectome's other lamina inputs. This is a result about the network, and is
  not a reason to raise G.
* **F3:** network health fails. The steep photoreceptor release then
  destabilises the network through feedback (photoreceptors receive synapses
  from lamina and medulla cells).
* **F4:** T4/T5 enter their band (falsifies P3). This is reported as a positive
  stage result, and the motion gate is then run.
* **F5:** the motion gate passes (falsifies P5). Stop, and report to the owner
  before the expensive protocol.
* **F6:** the instrument null control fails, only if the motion phase runs:
  R1–R6 D1 DSI ≥ 0.1 or R1–R6 D2 median ≥ 0.05 (v5's F5).
* **F7:** K0 and the primary disagree on a gate verdict. The verdict then
  rests on the kinetics table, and is reported as such.

## 10.8 The two gates, declared now

**STAGE GATE (cheap, run first, both arms).** Stimulus: the yaw CCW and yaw CW
conditions of §9.9. Statistic: per cell, the F1 amplitude at 1.5 Hz averaged

eye has only 300 lit cartridges (`WP5_OPTOMOTOR.md` §14.3). The population mean
is therefore mostly cells the stimulus does not reach, while p90 measures what
a stimulated cell does. The same statistic for v4, v5 and v5-S2, computed from
their committed raw files before this lock:

| right eye, p90 (mV) | R1-R6 | L1 | L2 | Mi1 | Tm3 | Tm1 | Tm2 | Mi9 | T4 | T5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v4 | 9.75 | 1.61 | 0.74 | 0.155 | 0.42 | 0.27 | 0.30 | 0.19 | 0.079 | 0.099 |
| v5 primary | 9.75 | 1.62 | 0.72 | 0.172 | 0.44 | 0.26 | 0.30 | 0.18 | 0.091 | 0.096 |

Bands, from §10.2:

* **Lamina: L1 and L2 each in [2, 25] mV.** Upper bound: the measured
  *Drosophila* LMC response range is 40–50 mV peak to peak, so the largest
  sinusoidal F1 it can support is ≈ 20–25 mV. Lower bound: one tenth of that.
  No identified L1/L2 whole-cell amplitude exists, and the LMC is band-pass in
  the animal (Nikolaev 2009: *Drosophila* LMC/R gain ≈ 0.6 at 2 Hz in bright
  light, ≈ 3–4 at middle intensity), so a 1.5 Hz response could legitimately
  sit low in the band.
* **T4/T5: T4 (a–d pooled) and T5 (a–d pooled) each in [1, 25] mV.** Lower
  bound: one fifth of the smallest measured T4/T5 visual response, the
  null-direction ≈ 5 mV (Gruntman 2018/2019; Groschner 2022). Upper bound: above
  every measured PD peak (≤ 25 mV).
* **Network health (gray window, primary and each arm):** brain mean rate in
  [0.5, 25] Hz (spikes per neuron per second over all 166,700, the v5
  statistic; brackets v4's 1.2 and v5's 10.4 with margin); graded cells at a
  membrane bound ≤ 5 % (v5: 0 %); **DNa02_L and DNa02_R** each firing in
  [0.5, 200] Hz, i.e. neither silent (v5-S2 was) nor near the 454 Hz
  refractory ceiling.

The stage gate passes only if every clause passes for the arm in question. **If
the primary's stage gate fails, STOP. The diagnostic is the deliverable**: the
per-stage amplitude trace against v4/v5, the health numbers, the release-clip
fractions, and the flicker timings. The motion phase is run for an arm only if
that arm's stage gate passes.

**MOTION GATE (only after a stage-gate pass): reused verbatim from §7.10 and
§9.9.** At least one T4/T5 subtype with D1 DSI ≥ 0.2 at response magnitude
≥ 0.5 mV, **and** DNa02 with yaw CCW giving L − R ≥ +1 Hz and yaw CW giving
L − R ≤ −1 Hz. Protocol: §9.9's corrected protocol, unchanged (settled gray,
3-cycle window, eight lattice directions, yaw, flicker), analysed by
`scripts/v6a_measurement/analyse_tuning.py`, which is v5's script with only its
default directory changed. **If the motion gate passes, STOP and report to the
owner before the optomotor validation protocol.** Whether to run it is the
owner's decision.

## 10.9 Measurements, cheap before expensive

Raw output goes straight into `docs/receipts/v6a_raw/` and is committed as each
run finishes. Every real-graph receipt records the card (CUDA device name and
PCI bus id, `nvidia-smi`), run with `CUDA_DEVICE_ORDER=PCI_BUS_ID
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1` (GTX 1660 Ti, bus 07:00.0).

**(a) Unit level, CPU, synthetic (`measure_unit.py`).** a1: small-signal DC
transfer of the reference pair. a2: static transfer curve. a3: dynamic
transfer at 1.5 Hz for 0.1 mV and 9.57 mV presynaptic sinusoids. a4: the same
for the default class. Both tables, both kinetics arms.

**(a′) Real-graph identity (`identity_real_graph.py`).** `v5-linear` against v5
under both kinetics tables, 200 ms of gray, plus a v5 repeat for the tolerance.

**(b) Stage phase (`measure_tuning.py --phase stage`).** 1,000 ms gray settle,
1,000 ms gray baseline, then yaw CCW, yaw CW and flicker, each from the saved
post-gray state (500 ms settle + 2,000 ms window). Run in this order: primary,
then K0. Analysed by `analyse_stage.py` against §10.8.

**(c) Motion phase, only after a stage pass (`--phase motion`).** The eight
lattice directions from the same saved gray state, then `analyse_tuning.py`.

## 10.10 What v6a does not claim

* It is not a photoreceptor-synapse model. It has one measured slope, from
  another genus, with no adaptation of the set point, no field-potential
  effect, no band-pass dynamics and no synaptic noise.
* The default class is unmeasured. Every graded synapse past the
  photoreceptors still transmits with v4's derived line.
* The bands are anchored on response scale, not on a protocol-matched
  measurement.
* A stage-gate pass would say the signal reaches T4/T5 at a biological scale.
  It would not say that the connectome computes direction. Only the motion gate
  addresses that.

## 10.11 If the stage gate fails: the next blocker, stated in advance

1. **Measured operating points and transfer of the lamina → medulla synapses**
   (L1→Mi1, L2→Tm1/Tm2, L3→Mi9). A gain above 1 there is meaningless without
   an anchor. An anchor needs either measured resting potentials per cell type
   or set-point adaptation, which is a mechanism, not a parameter.
2. **The connectome's weight scale.** The engine needs a release 2.4× steeper
   than the measured one to reach the measured gain (§10.4). The per-synapse
   PSP scale (0.275 mV, Shiu *et al.* 2024) was calibrated for spiking central
   neurons, not for tonic graded synapses.
3. v6b's input-cell temporal filtering (§9.11), which is still required for
   direction selectivity whatever the amplitudes are.
